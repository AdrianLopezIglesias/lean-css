A working tide

17 August 2026 · Mara Ellison · 12 min

The harbor does not open because someone typed ready into a console. It opens because the tide came in, the dock was swept, and someone counted the crates twice.

I have been walking the same mile of coast for a year. The work is ordinary. The light is not. Most mornings the water is the color of wet slate, and the only bright thing is a yellow sleeve of cups waiting on a pallet.

Keep the list short enough to finish before the weather changes.

— Dock board, unsigned

What the list is for

A list is not a plan. A list is a way to stop carrying the day in your head. When the wind picks up you want one next action, not a strategy deck.

I still write mine on paper. The first line is always a place. The second is a person. Software that forgets those two things is just a ledger with nicer type. 

place: north loop market
person: ada
next: leave the crate at the side door

After the walk

There is a bench above the breakwater. If you sit long enough the gulls stop performing. That is when the essay starts — not with a thesis, but with a quiet inventory of what actually moved.

Tomorrow I will walk the same mile. The tide will be different. The cups may still be short one sleeve.
